# Spelljammer To-Do

Perhaps without good reason, I'm including wildspace travel in my current campaign which has so far been ground-based with a tiny bit of planar travel.

While writing up my crystal sphere, [Iospace](https://github.com/DaneWeber/dragonvale/blob/main/locales/space/iospace.md), I want to figure out:

1. Why my PCs don't already know about wildspace and spelljamming ships.
2. Which world powers have access to wildspace, etc.

## Hypotheses

I need to check the rules to see if this is already addressed, but I'm imagining that larger bodies, such as my main planet of Iothar, should be harder to safely land on and launch from. My first searches suggest spelljamming and similar helms can fly in atmosphere just fine, with major implications for travel, trade, and military strategy.

What seems like a great solution for the things I'm weighing, where I wish to keep a planet surface that values naval shipping and protected ground-based caravans, is to make helms ineffective within atmosphere. Helms only produce propulsion when surrounded by Wildspace or Phlogiston. Similar in meta-game purpose to the Crystal Sphere separating Wildspace from Phlogiston, planetary bodies would have an atmospheric boundary that separates Wildspace from the planet's atmosphere. That barrier is called "the Drop" by spacefarers, and crossing it results in a sudden loss of propulsion. While there is atmosphere above the Drop, which reduces ships to tactical speed, the space below the Drop requires alternative means of propulsion and buoyancy control.

On what would be a usual main world, it would take a powerful airship to reach the Drop and transition from atmospheric to wildspace travel. A jamming helm alone is insufficient.

Given this, there could be specific ways to overcome the Drop:

1. Using the Ethereal to slowly ascend above the Drop before returning to the Material and engaging the jamming helm for wildspace travel.
2. Large-scale teleportation between an orbital platform and the planet's surface.
3. Something along the lines of a reverse-gravity zone, although winds might blow spacejammers out of the zone to plummet to the planet's surface.
