# Magic Rules

## Double-Casting

For spells that have a limited number of targets (such as Shadow Walk), a caster may cast multiple instances of the same spell together as a single casting in order to affect all of the valid targets at once. When doing so, the casting time increases dramatically: 10x per additional spell instance, starting at the greater of 1 round or the spell's normal casting time for the first instance. Casting two instances thus takes a minimum of 10 rounds (1 minute).

At the end of the casting time, the caster makes a single spellcasting check with a DC = 10 + Spell Level + 2 per spell instance. If the check fails none are affected and the spell slots are wasted. The caster may take 10 on this check unless threatened or distracted.

Because of the strain of this technique, once the spell's duration ends (instantly, after the normal duration, or when dismissed, etc.), the caster takes 1 point of damage per spell instance to their relevant casting ability (e.g., Intelligence for wizards, Charisma for sorcerers, etc.). This damage cannot be prevented or avoided in any way, although it is recovered normally.

Each instance of the spell uses up a spell slot as normal and the effects of each instance are resolved separately. For example, if two instances of Plane Shift are cast together in order to affect 9-16 targets, there will be two groups arriving on the new plane in two different locations.

If the spell can be dismissed, all instances are dismissed together.
